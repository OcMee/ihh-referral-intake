# Research: cost of delay, respite evidence, CalAIM, referral platforms, review time

Researched 2026-10-17 for the IHH referral-intake challenge. Public sources only. No patient information.

**How this was checked:** web search only. Direct page downloads were blocked by the event network, so
each figure below is taken from search-engine extracts of the named source, not from a full read of the
document. Before you put a number on a slide, open the URL and confirm it. Where sources disagree, both
are shown.

## 5-line summary for the pitch

1. A hospital day in California costs about **$4,471** (2023) in hospital expenses, vs **$3,132** US average (KFF / AHA adjusted expenses per inpatient day, an estimate, not a price).
2. Patients experiencing homelessness are readmitted within 30 days more often: **17.3% vs 14.0%** for housed patients across ~23 million hospitalizations in FL, MA and NY (Khatana et al., JGIM 2020).
3. Respite works best for heavy users: Boston respite patients had **3.7 vs 8.3 hospital days** in the next 12 months (Buchanan, AJPH 2006), and among frequently admitted Medicaid patients respite discharge was linked to **51% lower odds of 30-day readmission** (Racine, Medical Care 2020).
4. California DHCS reports recuperative care is associated with an **8.8% net cost reduction** (2026 fact sheet), and the number of recuperative care providers statewide grew from **97 to 224** (Q1 2022 to Q2 2024).
5. The big referral platforms (WellSky CarePort, Aidin, Ensocare) route, message and summarize referrals; WellSky's newest tool scores referrals against "configurable criteria" for completeness and fit, but **we found no published tool built for recuperative care rule sets**, and **no published figure for how long a manual respite packet review takes**.

## 2. Evidence that recuperative care / medical respite works

| Figure | What it measures | Source | Year | URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.7 vs 8.3 hospital days (P=.002) | Hospital days over 12 months after discharge, respite vs usual care. 225 homeless adults in Chicago; comparison group was referred but turned away because no bed was free. ## Not found / caveats

- **Not found:** how long a manual review of a recuperative care / medical respite packet takes. IHH's own numbers (packet length, staff time, 2-hour promise) are the best evidence; ask them.
- **Not found:** CalOptima or Orange County recuperative care utilization (members, stays, beds).
- **Not found:** CalOptima's specific required document list for recuperative care authorization. The Health Net and L.A. Care rules above are from other plans and may differ.
- **Not found:** statewide recuperative care member count on its own (only all-Community-Supports totals).
- **Not found:** referral abandonment rates for medical respite.
- **Not found:** California-specific 30-day readmission figures for homeless vs housed patients (a California neurology study reports an adjusted odds ratio of about 1.5, but we could not confirm the citation, so it is left out).
- KFF cost per day is hospital expenses, not what a payer pays, and includes outpatient adjustment.
- Readmission and respite effect figures come mostly from Massachusetts, Illinois, Florida, New York and Washington; none from Orange County.
- Respite evidence is mostly observational. The strongest effects are for frequently admitted patients; overall readmission effects are mixed.
- Vendor figures (WellSky, Aidin, Ensocare, LeadingReach) are marketing claims.
- All figures were read from search extracts because direct page fetches were blocked. Open each URL before quoting.

## Read from the original documents (uploaded by the team, Oct 17 2026)

These replace earlier search-summary claims where they differ.

**Recup Connect (Safety Net Connect + NIMRC).** Healthcare Innovation, David Raths, Oct 17 2025.
- Features: real-time bed availability and eligibility criteria; electronic referrals to preferred facilities or a shared pending queue for provider review; secure messaging between care settings.
- Scale at the time: 5 LA hospitals in the pilot (4 CommonSpirit + Huntington), 3 onboarded; "almost 1,000 referrals in the system", much of it entered by recuperative care facilities to track patients. (Earlier summary of "660+ referrals, 7 sites" came from a different, unverified source: do not use it.)
- Safety Net Connect also runs "the Cal-AIM platform for our local Orange County Medicaid plan called Cal Optima" (CEO quote). IHH may already use it.
- The article does not say the platform reads the clinical packet against the criteria; it shows each facility's criteria to the discharge planner.

**CarePort Referral Management (WellSky), brochure.** Receive referrals from hospitals in one system; notifications by text, fax or email; transfer demographic, financial and clinical data into back-office systems; share bed availability; configurable assessments; intake tasks; admit from a referral work list; mobile app. No criteria evaluation is described.

**Tennr.** Fierce Healthcare, Heather Landi, Jun 18 2025; IVP announcement, Jun 18 2025; NeuronFeed profile.
- Reads faxed referrals, extracts diagnosis, insurance, ordering provider, clinical justification; flags missing information; checks insurance eligibility; routes to schedulers.
- Its model RaeLM is "trained on 100 million anonymized healthcare documents, 2.3 billion distinct data fields and 8,000 sets of criteria" and "evaluates documents against" criteria (payer and qualification criteria; sentence cut off in our copy). **So Tennr does evaluate documents against criteria.** Our honest difference: it targets specialty practices, DME suppliers, labs and provider groups (insurance and scheduling), not a homeless recuperative care provider's admission rules.
- Scale: about 10 million documents a month (CEO, Fierce). Funding: $101M Series C at a $605M valuation; Fierce says $162M raised in total, NeuronFeed says $138M (conflict).
- Accuracy "over 99.8%" is IVP's (investor) claim, not independently verified.
- Fierce also reports an estimate that 70% of healthcare organizations still exchange information by fax (source of the estimate not shown).
